Helping non-experts ask better questions about market data

The AI Market Analyst pairs an interactive stock dashboard with a contextual Gemini assistant. Instead of making users interpret charts and headlines alone, the experience lets them explore an asset and ask plain-language follow-up questions about what they see.

Role Product Designer & Developer
Project Type Independent Prototype
Timeline Nov 2025 – Mar 2026
Tools Gemini, Dash, Plotly, yfinance
01 · Hypothesis

Charts show what changed, but not always what it means

Financial dashboards often assume that users already understand candlesticks, time horizons, company context, and the relationship between price movement and recent news. My working hypothesis was that non-expert users would benefit from explanations embedded directly beside the data they were exploring.

This is a product hypothesis, not a completed research finding. I built the prototype to make the interaction testable and to identify where AI adds value—or creates new trust problems.

Design question: How might an AI assistant explain the active market context without replacing the user's judgment or presenting generated text as financial advice?
02 · Why AI

Use generation for interpretation, not for the underlying facts

Contextual explanation

Generated responses can adapt explanations to the selected company and the user's specific question.

Follow-up dialogue

A conversational layer supports questions that fixed dashboard labels and tooltips cannot anticipate.

Grounded inputs

Market data and recent headlines come from external data sources; the model's role is to synthesize that context.

03 · Interaction

A single loop connects exploration and explanation

1

Select

Choose a sector, company, and time horizon.

2

Inspect

Review the candlestick chart, quote, and recent movement.

3

Ask

Pose a plain-language question about the active asset.

4

Continue

Use the response to ask a more specific follow-up or inspect another timeframe.

04 · Prompt Design

The model receives the same context the interface is showing

Each request combines four pieces of context: the active ticker, a company summary, recent news headlines, and the user's question. The system instruction asks Gemini to respond concisely, professionally, and in direct relation to that context.

Synchronizing the assistant with the selected asset prevents a common interaction failure: asking users to repeat information that is already visible elsewhere in the interface.

Current prompt structure

  • Identify the active ticker selected in the dashboard
  • Provide a live company summary and recent headline context
  • Include the user's question verbatim
  • Request a concise, contextual answer rather than a generic market overview
05 · Trust

A working prototype also exposes the risks that need design attention

The prototype demonstrates the core experience, but it does not yet solve every trust and safety concern. Generated explanations can be incomplete, overly confident, or based on stale context. A production version should make those limitations visible rather than hiding them behind a polished chat interface.

Source visibility

Show which company information and headlines informed the answer, including timestamps.

Uncertainty language

Distinguish sourced facts from interpretation and avoid implying guaranteed financial outcomes.

Failure recovery

Provide a useful fallback when market data, news, or model generation is unavailable.

06 · Validation

What is built—and what should be tested next

The dashboard, filtering, charts, data connections, and Gemini interaction are implemented as a working prototype. Formal usability testing has not yet been completed, so I do not claim improvements in comprehension or decision quality.

The next study should compare the dashboard with and without contextual AI assistance. I would measure explanation comprehension, confidence calibration, time to answer a market question, follow-up behavior, and whether users can distinguish facts from model interpretation.

Reflection: Adding AI is not the same as designing an AI experience. The product work is in deciding what context the model receives, how uncertainty is communicated, and where the user remains in control.
